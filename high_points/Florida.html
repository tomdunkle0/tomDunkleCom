Britton Hill (Florida)

Elevation: 375ft / 105m
Date Completed: July 21, 2026
Chronology: High point #44 of 50

Figure 1: The monument and two wooden benches marking Britton Hill.

Figure 2: Tom poses next to the monument marking Britton Hill.

Florida's high point is in a rural area in the panhandle that I visited as a last-ditch effort to make sure I added one new high point to my collection in 2026. The exact point is in Lakewood Park, along Walton County Road 285 just southeast of the border town of Florala, Alabama. It's "out there" enough that I didn't have cell service in the park at the time. A shocking horror!!

Figure 3: The sign marking Lakewood Park, with Tom's car and part of the park in the background.

Figure 4: The playground and covered picnic table at Lakewood Park.

Although small, the park is nice and well-maintained, especially considering how sparsely populated the surrounding communities seem. There is a set of three hiking trails, a monument marking Britton Hill, a playground, a restroom building and a covered, individual picnic table. The whole time I was there, nobody else showed up except for somebody in a truck who just photographed the sign but didn't get out (talk about commitment issues).

Figure 5: Walton County Road 285 in front of Lakewood Park, in cloudy weather during Tropical Storm Bertha (1:54pm). Two birds are visible in the sky, the second only if you zoom in.

Figure 6: Walton County Road 285 in front of Lakewood Park, during a sunny break in Tropical Storm Bertha (1:37pm).

But the reason for nobody else visiting was obvious: Tropical Storm Bertha was happening. I enjoyed this, because it provided a proper light 'hurricane' aesthetic for my Florida high point visit. I spent two hours in the park, during which the weather was constantly windy and rainy, with lots of cloud coverage and an occasional sunny break.

Figure 7: The front of the sign marking Britton Hill.

Figure 8: The back of the sign marking Britton Hill. The sign appeared to be new, celebrating the USA's 250th birthday (that same year).

The park boasted a fresh, new-looking sign marking Britton Hill. Not only were the colors bright, but a logo celebrated the USA's 250th birthday (which was 17 days before I visited). The sun is powerful in Florida, so I wouldn't be surprised if the colors on that sign fade quickly. Upon searching more online, I realized that this "America250FL Road Trip" is a program involving dozens of these marked locations around the state of Florida.

Figure 9: A view of one of the hiking trails in Lakewood Park.

Figure 10: Another view of one of the hiking trails in Lakewood Park.

The hiking trails in Lakewood Park were crawling with golden orb weavers (also known as banana spiders) on the day that I visited. I nearly ran right into the first one I saw, and then I noticed that there was a spider web nearly every ten feet along sections of the trail. This was my first time encountering them in the wild, and even as somebody who's not arachnophobic, it was pretty damn startling. The females are enormous, but they are harmless to humans.

Figure 11: A golden orb weaver, also known as a banana spider, on one of the hiking trails in Lakewood Park.

Figure 12: One of various high pointers' benches along the hiking trails in Lakewood Park.

The trails are also lined with several of the famous High Pointers' Foundation benches. I didn't take full advantage of them, and to be honest I don't always. But I do always take pictures of them.

Figure 13: One of the trailheads in Lakewood Park.

The trails were muggy. Hot and damp. I was wearing a rain jacket and trying not to run into spiders, so I was pouring sweat. To be honest, this type of forest felt foreign to me. Nothing like anywhere else I usually explore.
